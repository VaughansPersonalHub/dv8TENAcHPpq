We help you pick the right 3PL, write a contract that holds up, and fix the relationship when it goes wrong. No 3PL pays us a fee.

You want the outsourcing decision to suit how you actually operate. The wrong call gets expensive fast: a provider that underprices the labour, a contract you cannot get out of, a service level that drops in your first peak.

The job is to buy the right scope, on terms that hold, from a provider who can run your volumes day to day, not just win the tender.

LCA Savills is an independent logistics outsourcing consultant working across Asia. We run the full sequence with you, from needs assessment to a steady operation, and no provider funds the recommendation you act on.

Decide what you are outsourcing before you run a tender

The real question is what you are buying, not who is cheapest. Plenty of companies outsource transport and keep the warehouse in-house; plenty do the reverse.

Some volumes and service profiles do not suit a shared-user 3PL at all, so a dedicated site makes more sense. Shared-user means you share space, labour and equipment with other shippers. Dedicated means a site run only for you.

We map your network, volumes and service requirements against your cost base first. Skip that and every quote answers the wrong question.

A single regional rate rarely survives contact with the floor

Where you outsource changes what a sensible rate looks like. In Southern Malaysia you weigh mature warehouse stock against tighter labour supply; in other parts of Malaysia the land and cost picture reads differently again.

We build these market realities into the brief and cost model.

Stuck in a failing relationship? The contract could be the problem

When cost, service or trust has broken down, we come in as the independent party and work out what is actually wrong. It is often the contract rather than the operation. We help reduce your cost by helping reduce the costs of your supplier.

On one engagement the provider was hitting every SLA in the agreement, yet the client was still losing customers, because the SLAs measured the wrong things. The fix was a renegotiation and a KPI rewrite, not a re-tender.

Sometimes the answer is a managed exit and a fresh selection. Either way we represent you in the room, with the cost and service data to back what we ask for.

How we work

  1. Define the scope — What you outsource, and what good service looks like.
  2. Build the brief — Map network, volumes and service levels into a single comparable brief.
  3. Run the tender — Issue the RFP and run a structured process to a fixed timeline.
  4. Design the cost model — Structure bids so they are comparable, not just cheap on the surface.
  5. Shortlist on evidence — Live site visits and reference checks with operators who have run a peak.
  6. Negotiate the contract — Lock scope, pricing, KPIs and the exit clause before signature.
  7. Run the transition — Plan cutover, then govern performance after go-live.

Frequently asked questions

  • How do I choose the right 3PL in Asia?

    Define what you are outsourcing and what good service looks like, then take a structured brief to market so bids compare on cost and service, not just headline price. Watch out for ‘add-on costs’. Work collaboratively with vendors to find ways to reduce cost. Discussion matters. Shortlist on evidence: visit the warehouses the provider really runs, not the showcase site. Check references with operators who have been through a peak.

  • How long does a 3PL tender take in Asia?

    A structured tender usually runs 10 to 14 weeks from finalising the brief to signing, depending on how clean your data is and how many providers you shortlist. Add transition time on top. Rushing the front end is a false economy, because gaps in the brief surface later as variation orders and disputes. We ensure the 3PL can deliver operationally what they promise in the tender response.

  • What should a 3PL contract include?

    Scope defined down to the activity, a pricing model that survives volume swings, KPIs both sides can measure from real data, a review that forces year-on-year improvement, and a clean exit clause. The exit is the part most contracts skimp on and the part that gives you leverage when you need it.

  • Our 3PL relationship is failing. Do we have to re-tender?

    Not always. The fix is often a renegotiation and a reset of the KPIs rather than a full re-tender, especially when the contract or the data is the real problem rather than the operation on the floor. We diagnose what is broken first, then represent you in the room with the numbers.

  • Are you affiliated with any 3PL providers?

    No. We take no referral fees and run no provider panel, so we have no reason to steer you toward any particular 3PL. LCA represents the client, never the provider. That independence is the whole point of bringing in an outside consultant for selection and contracting.

  • What is the difference between open-book and fixed-price 3PL pricing?

    Open-book means the provider shows you the real cost lines and you pay an agreed management margin on top, so you see where your money goes. Fixed or closed-book means one agreed rate and the provider carries the risk if costs move. Open-book suits volatile or growing volumes; fixed suits stable, well-understood operations.

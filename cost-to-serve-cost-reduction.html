Cost-to-Serve: see what each customer really costs you

We build a detailed cost model that loads logistics cost onto every order, customer and product, then show you where to cut without hurting service.

Book a cost-to-serve review

Most companies know their average cost per case or per kilo. What they do not know is which customers, channels and SKUs are profitable once logistics is loaded on properly.

The answer is often uncomfortable. A customer, a category, or SKU that looks good on revenue or gross margin can be destroying value once you add the cost of small orders, distant delivery and frequent returns.

We build a cost-to-serve model from your actual operational data — warehouse labour, transport, inventory holding and order management — allocated to customers and products by the activities that actually drive cost.

What cost-to-serve reveals

A proper cost-to-serve model changes the conversation. It shows which customers you are subsidising, which channels make money and which do not, and which SKUs are profitable at the volumes you actually sell.

The usual pattern: 20% of customers generate 80% of profit, 30% break even, and 50% destroy value. Most leadership teams have never seen the bottom half laid out.

Once you see it, the decisions become obvious. Restructure pricing for unprofitable accounts. Consolidate delivery frequency. Drop or reformulate loss-making SKUs. The model gives you the numbers to have those conversations.

Rapid cost reduction: 12-week programmes

For clients who need speed, we run a structured 12-week cost reduction programme. Week 1-6: baseline and model build. Week 7-10: opportunity validation with the leadership team. Week 11-12: start implementation with weekly tracking. Exact timings depend on data availability.

Typical results: 15-25% logistics cost reduction, with service held or improved. The savings usually come from transport restructuring, warehouse productivity, and quite often an uplift in sales.

What we deliver

  • Activity-based cost model — Logistics cost loaded by customer, channel, SKU and order type.

Frequently asked questions

  • How is cost-to-serve different from standard costing?

    Standard costing spreads overheads evenly, so every case carries the same cost. Cost-to-serve traces actual activities — picking, packing, transport, order processing — to the customers and products that drive them. Two customers buying the same product at the same price can have very different profitability once logistics is loaded properly. Experience in end to end supply chain operations is key to building accurate cost-to-serve models that align stakeholders to take action.

  • How long does a cost-to-serve project take?

    A full model build takes 8 to 12 weeks. If you add a cost reduction implementation programme, the total is typically 12 to 16 weeks. We can tailor a diagnostic to focus on high-priority areas so that implementation can commence early, with the actions delivering benefits sooner.

  • What data do you need?

    Example information that we need includes customer master data, product master, order and delivery data, warehouse and transport data. If some of this is hard to get, we work with what you have and jointly estimate sensibly where necessary.

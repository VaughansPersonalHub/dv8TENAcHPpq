Get inventory right for how you actually run. Service improves, working capital comes down, and you stop paying for stock that hides problems you could fix instead.

Most inventory problems are not solved by holding more stock. They are solved by sizing safety stock to the demand variability and lead times you actually have.

Then by sorting your SKUs so fast and slow lines get different rules, and positioning stock across your network instead of holding everything everywhere.

LCA Savills does that work across Asia. We have run these operations ourselves, and we answer to your numbers.

Why over-buffering inventory costs you twice

Safety stock has a job: it absorbs the variability you cannot predict. The trouble is most businesses set it once and never touch it as suppliers and demand shift, so it creeps up. You pay for that buffer in working capital and warehouse space.

You pay a second time because it hides what you should be fixing: a weak forecast, suppliers who are reliably late. A vendor in Vietnam who runs two weeks behind looks fine when a month of cover sits in front of him.

Strip the buffer back to what the data justifies. You usually free up cash and expose the lead-time and forecast problems worth fixing.

Demand forecasting your planners will actually use

A forecast only helps if the planning team trusts it and acts on it. We look at what drives your demand, which SKUs are forecastable and which are not, and where promotions, seasonality or a few big customers distort the picture.

Then we build a forecasting approach matched to that reality, set accuracy targets that mean something, and put a monthly review in place so the number improves instead of drifting. The aim is not a cleverer model. It is a number the business will commit to.

Frequently asked questions

  • How do you reduce inventory without hurting service?

    We size safety stock to the variability you actually see, not a textbook formula. Then we segment SKUs so fast lines get tighter control and slow lines get less attention. Positioning stock closer to demand reduces lead-time exposure. The result is usually lower inventory and better service, because the stock that remains is in the right place.

  • How long does an inventory project take?

    A typical engagement runs 6 to 10 weeks: data gathering and analysis in weeks 1-3, policy design and validation in weeks 4-6, and implementation support through weeks 7-10. We stay until the new policies are running and your team is confident.

  • Do we need new software?

    Usually not. Most inventory improvements come from better policy, segmentation and process, not new technology. If your current system genuinely cannot support what you need, we will say so and help you specify requirements without favouring any vendor.

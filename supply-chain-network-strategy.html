What a network decision actually locks in

A network is a set of decisions: how many sites you run, where they sit, how big each is, and which markets each one serves.

The decisions are physical once made. A lease signed and racking bolted to the floor are hard to walk back, so the cost of getting it wrong runs for years.

Designing a network involves making decisions such as network resilience, speed to customer, fixed versus variable cost commitments, stock holding alternatives, and the supporting technology. And designing a pan-Asian network can make these decisions even harder than a single-country network. For example, a DC in Johor serving Singapore and Thailand crosses borders that each carry their own duties, documentation and clearance times.

We work the landed cost to serve, not the rent

You decide where to put a DC by demand location and total cost to serve, not by rent per square metre.

A node that looks cheap on rent loses that edge the moment you add freight costs, transport delays and the buffer stock you hold to cover it. So we model the total cost to serve for each option along with the qualitative factors that are important to making the network work.

Frequently asked questions

  • How do I decide where to locate a distribution centre domestic or regional in Asia?

    Start with where your demand sits and the full cost to serve each location, not the rent. Factors such as transport cost, network security, lead time to customers, service standards are all factors which influence location – not just the cheapest square metre.

  • How many distribution centres do I need?

    Usually fewer than most companies assume. Every extra DC adds fixed cost and splits your inventory, so each one has to earn its place by cutting freight or improving service enough to justify it. We model central versus regional structures against your real demand, then recommend the number that holds up if volumes come in higher or lower than plan.

  • Will you recommend a building you also profit from selling?

    No. Our fee does not change with which site you pick, so we have nothing to gain by steering you to one property over another. The recommendation rests on the model and the numbers.

  • Do cross-border duties and lead times really change the network answer?

    Often, yes. A node that looks cheap can lose its edge once you add customs delays and the safety stock you hold to cover them. Duty treatment between markets, free-zone status and clearance reliability all feed into where stock should sit. We build these into the model rather than treat them as a footnote, because in this region they routinely decide the outcome.

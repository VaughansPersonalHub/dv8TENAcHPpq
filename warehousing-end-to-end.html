Where warehouse projects bleed money: the gaps between disciplines

A new DC touches site selection, design, automation, procurement, SOPs, shift design, training and migration. The gaps between those processes are where time and money leak.

Site selection sits with property people. Design goes to an engineering firm. Automation goes to a vendor whose interests are not yours. The rest lands on a client’s team who already have full-time jobs.

We hold the whole thing together and represent one interest, yours, from the first site shortlist to the day your team takes over a DC that hits its numbers.

Built country by country, because what is buildable changes at every border

"Asia" is a generic term. The work is country by country, because permitting, work styles, land tenure and labour rules change at every border and sometimes at every state line.

We know what is actually buildable on each shortlisted site before anyone commits, and we factor cross-border corridors when your network spans more than one market.

Frequently asked questions

  • What does a client’s representative do on a warehouse project in Asia?

    We manage the design, build and commissioning of your facility while representing only your interests. In practice that means pressure-testing the design, running procurement, and holding contractors and automation vendors to the signed contract, checking every change order before you pay for it.

  • What is a built-to-suit warehouse and how does it differ from leasing a spec-build?

    A built-to-suit warehouse is purpose-built around your throughput, layout and automation, rather than a standard shed you adapt after the fact. Leasing an existing building is faster and cheaper up front but usually forces compromises on layout, automation choices, ceiling height and dock count. We help you weigh both honestly against your real volumes.

  • Can I hire LCA Savills for just part of a warehouse project?

    Yes. The service is modular. If you already have a building and a developer, we can do the parts that are usually under-resourced, such as an independent design review, procurement, WMS requirements, training or stock migration. You can take the full programme or a single workstream.

  • Does LCA Savills run the warehouse after it is built?

    No. Our role runs from site to go-live and stabilisation, then handover to your team.

  • How long does it take to design and build a distribution centre in Asia?

    It depends on whether you are fitting out an existing building or doing a built-to-suit, and on local permitting, which varies by country and site. A fit-out can take months; a purpose-built facility runs longer once you factor in land, zoning and construction. Overall, it can take up to 2 – 3 years. The honest answer comes after the site assessment.
